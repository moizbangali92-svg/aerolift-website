Jib Cranes

Jib Cranes: Wall or Pillar Mounted, Covering the Workstation

Where one machine, one bench or one loading point does the same lift all day, a jib crane covers it — without tying up the bay crane or building a runway.

The configuration decision

Wall or Pillar: Where Does the Crane Stand?

The arc the arm sweeps and the floor space it costs — that is the trade between the two mountings.

Wall-Mounted Jib

Bolted to a wall or column you already have — rotating 180° to 200° across the workstation.

Choose it when

  • The workstation sits against a wall or structural column
  • Floor space is too valuable to give up to a pillar base
  • A half-circle of coverage genuinely covers the work

Pillar-Mounted Jib

Free-standing on its own baseplate and foundation — a full 360° of rotation, positioned wherever the work is.

Choose it when

  • The lift point sits away from any wall — mid-floor, by a machine
  • The work needs the full circle — load on one side, set down on the other
  • No structure nearby is fit to carry a wall bracket

Repetitive lifts are exactly where a jib earns its keep — the free site visit confirms reach, arc and mounting before anything is ordered.

Sizing it properly

Four Things Decide the Right Jib

  • 01.

    Capacity

    The heaviest piece the station handles — with the margin the duty deserves.

  • 02.

    Reach

    Arm length from the pivot to the furthest pick point. Every extra metre is leverage the mounting must resist.

  • 03.

    Rotation arc

    180°–200° against a wall, 360° on a pillar — the arc has to cover pick-up and set-down.

  • 04.

    Mounting structure

    The wall, column or foundation takes the whole moment — it gets engineered, not assumed.
