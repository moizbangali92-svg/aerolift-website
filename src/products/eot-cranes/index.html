The configuration decision

Single or Double Girder: The First Question That Matters

Both run on the same elevated runway. The difference is what sits between the end carriages — and what that means for load, hook height and the life the crane will lead.

Single Girder EOT

One bridge beam, with the hoist running on its bottom flange. The economical answer for most covered bays.

Choose it when

  • Loads are light to medium and the lifting rhythm is moderate
  • The bay is a workshop or warehouse doing general lifting
  • Budget matters and the duty cycle does not justify more steel

Double Girder EOT

Two bridge beams with the trolley running on top. More capacity, more hook height, more crane.

Choose it when

  • Duty cycles are heavy — shift after shift, lift after lift
  • The span is long, or every metre of hook height counts
  • The load itself is heavy enough that the top-running trolley earns its keep

Not sure which side of the line your bay falls on? That is exactly what the free site visit settles — an engineer measures the bay and recommends the configuration, in writing.

Sizing it properly

Four Numbers Decide the Right Crane

An EOT crane specified on the wrong numbers is either an upgrade twelve months in or steel you paid for and never use. These are the four we ask about first.

  • 01.

    Span

    Rail to rail across the bay. It drives the girder, and the girder drives almost everything else.

  • 02.

    Capacity

    The heaviest lift the crane must make — not the average one. Rated for the worst day, not the typical day.

  • 03.

    Duty cycle

    How often, how heavy, how many hours a day. The number most often guessed — and the most expensive to guess wrong.

  • 04.

    Headroom

    The space between the rail and the roof. It decides the hoist configuration — and sometimes the girder type.
